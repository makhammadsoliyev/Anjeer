## 0. Summary

1. **The home market already has three gamified consumer platforms for school children, and none of
   them publishes evidence.** Ziyoly (operator Testbor, Tashkent) is an adaptive maths/logic/English
   trainer for grades 1–11 with a diagnostic, daily goals, achievements, rankings, two in-app currencies
   (ZCoin, TCoin) and a shop, a parent app and a daily Telegram report; it claims 525,000 users and
   26 million exercises, has 100,000+ Google Play installs rated 4.9 from 1,840 reviews, and charges
   500,000 UZS a month (≈ $40), 4,200,000 a year. Qalan's Uzbek version pays 2,000 UZS cashback per
   correctly solved example and claims 1.5 bn UZS paid out; its Uzbek-storefront reviews (4.1 from
   4,580 ratings globally; 3.2 from 5 on the Uzbek App Store) are about confusion and nerves, not about
   the money. Yooksal (Global Move) gates each test behind 90 % of a video and each lesson behind a 60 %
   test, shows parents a traffic light and sends an SOS after a week of inactivity, and sells modules
   at 250,000–300,000 UZS. All three are younger than three years and all three count registrations.
2. **"Money for marks" has arrived in Uzbekistan through the e-diary.** Since 4 September 2025 eMaktab
   (ex-Kundalik, the national e-diary with 5 million+ installs) and Hamkorbank run a loyalty programme
   in Tashkent, Samarkand and Urgench: a Hamkorbank card gives the parent a free PRO subscription and
   "points for the child's good marks" redeemable with partners in an "eMaktab.Bonus" window. Thresholds
   and caps are not published. It is Kundelik's K.Bonus (KZ note §2.4) one year later, bank-funded, and
   again it rewards the mark, not the learning.
3. **The state pays for outcomes at the top and, from 2027, for primary pupils too.** International
   olympiad medals pay pupils 500/300/200 BHM (≈ 170/102/68 mln UZS), raised five-fold from June 2025
   (gold 2,500 BHM); republic- and regional-stage winners of the main subject olympiads get a 3-day or
   2-day free trip under a July 2026 regulation, and from 2027 district-stage winners of a **new
   4th-grade olympiad get a computer**, their teachers 10/50/100 mln UZS by stage. "Yosh kitobxon"
   gives a car to the best young readers (BYD Chazor in 2025; 75 and 50 mln UZS for 2nd and 3rd place,
   10 mln to every finalist). The ministry's own online-olympiad portal runs quarterly online rounds in
   five subjects for grades 4–11 with an automatic ranking. Prizes in Uzbekistan are large, rare and
   state-funded; nothing in between rewards a week of ordinary practice.
4. **The Presidential-school segment is bigger per place than Kazakhstan's and sells almost no
   software.** 60,169 children registered for 2026/27 against 336 places (≈ 179 per place if the quota
   is unchanged; 49,924 applied in 2025); 480 per region pass to stage 2. Specialised schools took
   109,560 applicants for about 8,200 grade-5 places. The prep market is Telegram channels (@pmtests,
   79,100 subscribers) that run free timed tests through a bot and post ranked lists of children's full
   names, offline chains (Newton Academy: 3 × 3 hours a week for 10 months, weekly curator messages, a
   "motivation system" for homework, no public price) and a handful of sites that publish no price and
   convert through a "free consultation". The two sites named in the overview, testpm.uz and orbital.uz,
   did not respond or no longer resolve on 2026-10-20.
5. **The best causal evidence in the country is a weekly trophy, not a coin.** The UNICEF/MoPSE Eduten
   pilot (1,046 grade-5 pupils, 17 Tashkent schools, 12 weeks, comparison group) measured +16.9 % in
   maths and +10.2 % in arithmetic fluency, Cohen's d = 0.42. Its only "gamification" is a weekly trophy
   for completing 50/70/90/100 % of the assigned set (77/60/51/44 % of pupils reached each level in an
   average week), 1 h 24 min a week on task, 73 % of it on phones, much of it at home. An MoU with the
   ministry followed on 31 October 2025. The DTM segment (TestMakon, SharqTest, BirPrep) copies XP,
   badges, duels and weekly leaderboards from the global playbook with no evidence at all.
6. **Parents complain about price, logins and billing, never about a coin or a streak.** eMaktab.Oila's
   most-upvoted October 2026 reviews call it "birtinga qimmat" (expensive for nothing) and a way to
   "make money off the people"; Ziyoly's lowest reviews say "pulli ekan" (it turned out to be paid) and
   report phone-number friction; Qalan's say "I don't understand what to do" and "my nerves, one mistake
   and it adds more"; Ibrat Academy's (state, 1 mln+ installs) say "only one lesson a day and it asks for
   a subscription for everything". The pattern of the RU and KZ notes holds at home.
7. **The market is small, young and Telegram-shaped.** Smart Ranking puts online education at ≥ 126 bn
   UZS in 2024 (+94 %), with children's online education at 33.6 bn (+1,095 %, 28 % of the market, from
   a tiny base); the average salary is ≈ 2.1 mln UZS a month against courses priced up to 12 mln, and
   instalment approval tightened in 2024. 70 %+ of the population uses Telegram; 69 % of schools have no
   Wi-Fi; 99 % of people over 10 have a mobile phone. Every platform found signs up or supports through a
   Telegram bot. The price anchors for a child's monthly practice are 15,000 UZS (eMaktab PRO), 40,000
   (TestMakon Premium), 500,000 (Ziyoly Premium) and 250,000–300,000 per 4–6-week module (Yooksal).

## 2. Platform profiles

### 2.1 Ziyoly (Testbor) — grades 1–11; the local adaptive trainer with two coins

**What it is.** An adaptive online platform for grades 1–11 "designed for independent study at home",
operated by Testbor (testbor.uz, info@testbor.com, +998 55 507 5000, mentor bot @testbor_mentor_bot);
Google Play developer "Ziyoly Ta'lim platformasi" (app id com.testbor). Subjects: mathematics
(1,500+ skills), logical thinking, critical thinking, English; content in Uzbek, Russian and English;
"only in Uzbekistan for now", physics/chemistry/biology and SAT/GRE planned. Companion apps: **Ziyoly
Parent** (1,000+ installs, April 2026) and **Mock24 UZ** (500+ installs, "real exam simulation with
AI-powered grading, official question bank"). Claims: "525,000+ users", "26 mln+ maths exercises
completed", "3,000+ topics", "a billion+ interactive exercises" (generated variants).

**The loop (from the Play listing and site, 2026-10-20).**

| Layer | Mechanic | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | Diagnostic test → level → recommended learning path ("yo'l xaritasi") | about-us, Play |
| Daily norm | "15–20 minutes a day is enough"; **daily goals** ("kunlik maqsadlar"), daily reports | about-us, Play |
| Practice | Exercise types: single/multi-choice, fill-in, matching and ordering, formula/calculation, picture questions, open answers; "if the child errs, the system explains and offers similar new examples until understood" | Play |
| Progress | "Smart Score", mastery level, growth dynamics, personal progress | Play |
| Rewards | **Achievements, rankings, ZCoin and TCoin** (two currencies, with a history screen) and a **shop** ("do'kondagi buyurtma") — what the coins buy is not published | Play "What's new" (Oct 2026) |
| Competition | "maths battles, challenge-based games and online olympiads" | about-us |
| AI | "AI Mentor" chat with image and voice messages (added Oct 2026) | Play |
| Parent | Ziyoly Parent app; **Premium includes a daily Telegram report to the parent** ("what the child learnt, how many exercises") | subscription page |

**Prices (subscription page, 2026-10-20).** Premium 1 month 500,000 UZS; 2 months 800,000 (−20 %);
6 months 2,250,000 (−25 %); 12 months 4,200,000 (−30 %); promo codes; refund window 5 days for a
1-month plan (terms). Premium = unlimited exercises, explanations and tests in maths, geometry, logic and
English. The free tier exists (reviews: "juda zo'r ilova ekan ammo pulli ekan" — "great app but it
turned out to be paid"). Internet traffic is explicitly excluded from the price (terms).

**Reviews (Google Play, Uzbek storefront).** 4.9 from 1,840 ratings, "#7 top free education"; the
visible October 2026 reviews are a 4th-grader-style "after installing I am first to finish maths homework
every day" (25 upvotes), a login complaint ("after the password it asks for a phone number"), and the
"paid" complaint; the developer answers within three days and asks for five stars. Parent testimonials on
the site are the Qalan/Ziyoly genre: "my son is in grade 4, finished grade-7 maths in 10 months", "we
paid a lot to a tutor before".

**Why it matters for Anjeer.** Ziyoly is the local product closest to Anjeer's practice loop (diagnostic
→ adaptive items → explanation → parent line), it already uses *two* coins and a shop, and it prices a
child's month at 500,000 UZS, above what the overview expected families to pay. It has no teacher or
center side and no exam-specific bank beyond Mock24; its evidence is testimonials and a 4.9 rating.

### 2.2 Qalan in Uzbekistan — grades 1–11, maths; cash per correct example

The Kazakh mechanics are in the KZ note §2.1; this is only the Uzbek localisation.

- **Entity and site.** "QALAN" MChJ, Tashkent (Labzak 64A), +998 55 510 90 90; qalanedu.uz (Uzbek,
  Latin script), qalan.uz (JS shell). Sign-up is a "free consultation" form, not a checkout.
- **Cashback rule as stated in Uzbek:** "2,000 so'm keshbek for each correctly solved example",
  "mistakes are not punished"; total "1 billion 500 mln so'm cashback" paid (another page: 1.3 bn). The
  recipient is not stated on the Uzbek pages; in Kazakhstan the money goes to the parent's card.
- **Counts conflict between two builds of the same page:** "14,000+ students in Uzbekistan, 1 mln
  worldwide, 2.5 years in Uzbekistan" versus "8,800+ in Uzbekistan, 60,000 worldwide, 1 year in
  Uzbekistan"; the info page says "40,000 students, 20,000 parents, 2,000 video lessons".
- **App stores.** Google Play (kz.qalan.app): 4.1 from 4,580 ratings, 500,000+ installs (all
  markets), description "15–20 tasks a day", "grades 0–11, 30,000 successful learners". Uzbek App Store:
  3.2 from 5 ratings, chart #130 Education; In-App Purchases.
- **Uzbek reviews (Oct 2026, Play).** "tushunib bo'lmaydi … shaxsiy o'qitishda nima qilishni
  bilmayapman" (can't understand it, don't know what to do in self-study; 29 upvotes); "Asab bitta hato
  qisa qo'shaveradi" (my nerves, one mistake and it keeps adding tasks); "qanday ishlatishni bilmadim"
  (didn't know how to use it; 18 upvotes). App Store: SMS codes arrive only via WhatsApp; one Russian
  review "teachers call every day if the child hasn't done the lessons; the child learnt discipline";
  one "if you study more than a month you get tired of the process".
- **Positioning.** "The first online maths platform in Uzbekistan"; Presidential schools are *not*
  mentioned on the Uzbek site (they are in the Uzbek App Store description quoted in the KZ note).

Reading: the cash mechanic travelled; the discipline it funds (a generated daily norm, mentors who
call) is what the Uzbek reviews react to, positively and negatively. The complaints are onboarding and
pressure, not the reward.

### 2.3 Yooksal / Yuksal.uz (Global Move) — grades 2–9; gated video → test loop, parent traffic light

**What it is.** A Tashkent platform by Global Move (also UzChess, LittleChess, "My School Online",
"Waqt: Focus Screen Time"), relaunched with a new brand in September 2026; web app, Google Play
(uz.globalmove.yooksal, 5,000+ installs, 4.5 from 13 reviews, Sept 2026), App Store. Tracks: Presidential
schools (maths, logic, English, mock at the end of each module), Al-Khwarizmi schools, specialised
schools; courses by grade: grades 2–3 "Boshlang'ich matematika", 3–4 "English Grammar" and "Math-Up",
5–6 "Math Pro". Teacher named in testimonials: Asror Jumanazarov, who also founded @pmtests (§2.8).

**The loop.** Video lesson → test → video analysis of the test → work on mistakes → "AI recommendation"
of the next topic. **A topic's test opens only after 90 % of the video is watched; a test below 60 %
blocks the next lesson** (pass mark 60–70 %). Timer, exit warning, mock exams per module, 4 lessons + 4
tests a week, "10 months · 144 lessons". **Parent mode:** traffic-light activity status (green / yellow /
red), results, payment status, weekly report, and an **SOS signal when the child has not logged in for
over a week**. "Reyting" and "Mock testlar" anchors exist but are empty. Live quizzes ("Bilimlar jangi",
182 pupils in one round) run through the Telegram channels. No coins, streaks or badges.

**Prices.** Per module: 250,000 UZS (Boshlang'ich matematika, English Grammar; 5 modules each) and
300,000 (Math Pro, 6 modules; Math-Up, 5 modules); Click and Payme. No free trial stated. Claims: "32,000+
students, 30,000+ parents, 300+ teachers" (testimonial counters). One parent testimonial mentions a
"160 million so'm prize olympiad" with no rules on the site.

**Reviews.** "AI agent shows my wrong answers, but the tests are few and ran out quickly" (Oct 2026).

Reading: Yooksal is the Uzbek instance of the "no fun layer, hard gating, parent dashboard" school of
design; the SOS-after-a-week is the one novel parent mechanic found in this market.

### 2.4 eMaktab (ex-Kundalik) — context, plus the eMaktab.Bonus scheme

- **What it is.** The national e-diary, Kundalik LLC under a public-private partnership with the
  Ministry of Preschool and School Education; renamed from Kundalik.com to eMaktab in 2023; Wikipedia
  "3 million+ registered users"; **eMaktab.Oila** (family app) 5 mln+ installs, 4.0 from 70,500 reviews,
  "#1 top free education" on the Uzbek Play store; sister apps eMaktab.Jurnal (teachers) and eMaktab.Treker.
- **Pricing history (the market's longest-running paywall).** Free until November 2020, then 9,200 UZS
  a year per child; 8,600 (2021); 8,000 (2022/23) with a PRO tier; now basic web access per child plus
  **PRO 15,000 a month / 60,000 a year**, one PRO covers up to six family members; one free month to try.
  Official line: "paying is voluntary; schools and the company do not take the money" (FAQ); in December
  2021 the ministry had to state that "no instruction was given to force parents to renew".
- **Reviews, October 2026 (most upvoted).** "birtinga qimmat … login parolni to'g'ri kiritsam ham kirib
  bo'lmaydi" (expensive for nothing; can't log in even with the right password; 48 upvotes); a long
  complaint that the operator "found one more way to make money … let nobody use it" (32 upvotes); one
  praise "we see the child's grades, very convenient" (11 upvotes). The 2020–22 press pattern (outages,
  "does a paid diary violate free education") continues in the store.
- **eMaktab × Hamkorbank (4 September 2025).** Pilot in Tashkent, Samarkand and Urgench: a Hamkorbank
  card enrols the parent in the bank's loyalty programme; the parent gets **PRO at no extra cost**;
  **points are awarded "for the child's good marks"** and exchanged in an **"eMaktab.Bonus"** window for
  partner discounts. No mark thresholds, point values or caps are published. This is the first
  bank-sponsored "bonuses for grades" scheme in Uzbekistan and it mirrors Kundelik K.Bonus (KZ).
- **2026/27 (sponsored article, Gazeta.uz 25 Aug 2026).** Integration with erp.maktab.uz (the ministry's
  school ERP), ready thematic plans, "25 % of tasks automated", **"eMaktab payments"** pilot in Tashkent
  private schools from September 2026 with instalments and card privileges, AI tools for teachers
  (assistant, lesson constructor, automatic marking, voice journal), 20 pilot engineering classes. No
  gamification for pupils.

Reading: eMaktab holds the parent relationship for 5 million+ families and is moving into payments and
bank partnerships. Its reward scheme rewards the mark; its reviews show that a paywall in front of the
child's grades is as unpopular here as in Kazakhstan.

### 2.5 Eduten pilot (UNICEF / MoPSE, 2023–24) — the only controlled study; a weekly trophy

- **Design.** 17 public schools in Tashkent, 1,046 grade-5 pupils: 527 replaced one weekly maths lesson
  (45 min + homework) with Eduten for 12 weeks (spread over 15 with a break), 519 in the same schools
  continued as usual. Pre/post: a MoPSE curriculum test and a University of Turku 3-minute arithmetic
  fluency test. Three-day teacher training. 744 exercises localised (gaps filled for currencies and
  angles). Report June 2025 (Kaila & Kurvinen; peer-reviewed within UNICEF).
- **Results.** +16.9 % maths skills, +10.2 % arithmetic fluency; **Cohen's d = 0.42** on the maths test
  (the report compares it with ALEKS's meta-analytic 0.05). Teachers 4.7/5 for national adoption;
  parents' attitude 4.5/5 (teacher-reported).
- **Mechanics.** No coins. A **weekly trophy**: bronze for completing ≥ 50 % of the week's assigned
  exercises, silver 70 %, gold 90 %, diamond 100 %; "failure to earn bronze ≈ not doing the homework".
  Average share of pupils reaching each level in a week: **77 % bronze, 60 % silver, 51 % gold, 44 %
  diamond**. Points per exercise, a weekly score (recommended minimum 300), accuracy band 70–90 % as the
  target difficulty, pair mode (two pupils, one device).
- **Usage.** 10,411 hours on task over 15 weeks; **1 h 24 min a week per pupil on average** (54 min to
  nearly 2 h by school) against a 45-minute lesson, i.e. heavy home use; two activity peaks a day from
  two-shift schooling; Friday most popular; **73 % of sessions on mobile devices**, mostly Android;
  15 of 17 schools "well equipped", two low-resourced; after a parents' event "many caregivers allowed
  the family device or bought one". Weekly score declined after week 3 ("workload optimisation") and in
  the last two weeks ("exercises exhausted").
- **Limitations named by the authors.** Novelty effect, 12 weeks is short for teachers, self-report
  surveys, test reliability (two pre-test items dropped; some fluency tests not timed), no randomisation
  of individuals, Tashkent only.
- **After the pilot.** MoU between Eduten and MoPSE signed 31 October 2025 at the Presidential Palace
  during the Finnish state visit; the ministry "is assessing infrastructure, curriculum alignment and
  funding pathways" before any full deployment. No 2026 scale-up figures found.

Reading: the strongest Uzbek evidence is for a completion norm with a visible weekly threshold and
teacher follow-up, the same shape as Sparx/Reflex/Qalan, and it worked on phones at home. The ministry
knows this product; a center-side tool that looks like it will be understood.

## 5. Lessons the home market has paid for

1. **A completion norm with a visible weekly threshold is the only thing proven here.** Eduten's bronze
   at 50 % of the week's set, earned by 77 % of pupils, with teacher follow-up and home use on phones, is
   what produced d = 0.42. Coins, cash and leagues have no Uzbek evidence at all.
2. **Families already pay 250,000–500,000 UZS a month for a child's maths practice** (Ziyoly Premium,
   Yooksal modules) and 15,000–40,000 for utilities (eMaktab PRO, TestMakon). The overview's "no public
   B2C price data" (§3.2) is no longer true; these are the anchors for menu 1.
3. **Telegram is the sign-up, support and parent channel for every product found**, including the
   state's. A password-less bot login (TestMakon), a mentor bot (Ziyoly), a results bot (@pmtests) and a
   daily parent line (Ziyoly) are the local norm; menu 4b's Telegram bot is table stakes, not a feature.
4. **Onboarding, not the reward, is where children drop.** The three lowest Qalan reviews and two of
   three Ziyoly reviews are "I don't understand what to do", "it asks for a phone number", "one mistake
   and it adds more". A first session that a 10-year-old can finish alone matters more than any coin.
5. **Never paywall the parent's view of the child, and never sell it with a bank card.** eMaktab's
   reviews repeat Kundelik's nine-year lesson; eMaktab.Bonus adds a new risk, a child's marks feeding a
   bank's loyalty programme. Anjeer's parent mastery view belongs in the seat.
6. **Public ranked lists of children by name are normal here and still wrong for Anjeer.** Channels and
   the ministry portal publish them; a consent-based product for 9–11-year-olds cannot (constitution,
   overview §0 consent model). Any ranking must be class-internal, opt-in and attempts-based.
7. **Hard gating sells to parents and starves the child of items.** Yooksal's 90 %-video and 60 %-test
   locks are the prep segment's idea of rigour; the first review complains the tests "ran out". With a
   finite approved bank and an exposure rule, Anjeer must not gate on video watch-time.
8. **The Presidential funnel has a fixed cut (480 per region), not a published score.** "Distance to the
   cut-off" (KZ option BE) can only be expressed as a rank within the center's own cohort or a mock
   percentile, labelled as such.
9. **The state is extending outcome prizes to grade 4 in 2027 and knows Eduten.** A 4th-grade olympiad
   with a computer for district winners will shape what parents of Anjeer's cohort call a reward; a
   center that can say "your child's practice record is Eduten-shaped" speaks the ministry's language.
10. **Count installs, not registrations, when sizing competitors.** Every local vendor's counter is 5–10×
    its install band; Ziyoly's 100,000+ installs and Qalan's 8,800–14,000 Uzbek learners are the
    realistic scale of the "adaptive maths at home" market today.

## 7. What is specific to Anjeer's context

- **The competitor map in `project-overview.md` §3.2 is out of date.** testpm.uz and orbital.uz are
  unreachable; Ziyoly (adaptive grades 1–11, two coins, daily parent Telegram line, 500,000 UZS a
  month), Yooksal (Presidential track, gated loop, parent SOS, 250,000–300,000 per module) and Qalan's
  Uzbek entity are not listed. Newton Academy's format (3 × 3 h a week, 10 months, weekly curator
  message, monthly regrouping) is the offline product Anjeer's first center competes with and resembles.
- **Price anchors for menu 1 exist now.** Child practice: 500,000/month (Ziyoly), 250,000–300,000 per
  module (Yooksal), 40,000/month (TestMakon, ages 16–18); utilities: 15,000/month or 60,000/year
  (eMaktab PRO); teacher tools: 25,000/month (Testkor Pro), 3,000 per student per month (Testkor
  Markaz). A center's per-child "learner seat" will be compared with Testkor's 3,000 and a family's
  subscription with Ziyoly's 500,000 and eMaktab's 15,000.
- **Telegram is already decided (menu 4b) and the market confirms it**: bot login without password
  (TestMakon), mentor bot, results bot, daily parent line (Ziyoly Premium), weekly curator message
  (Newton), SOS after seven idle days (Yooksal). The templated parent messages of AI order 8d are the
  local standard, not a differentiator; the content (mastery, misconception names) is.
- **The reward unit question (KZ §9 Q1) has a home-market answer in the data.** Eduten's weekly
  completion trophy at 50/70/90/100 % is the only Uzbek mechanic with a measured effect; it maps onto
  the Leitner queue (menu 7) as "cards closed this week", not minutes or accuracy.
- **Consent model vs market habit.** The market publishes children's names and scores; eMaktab now links
  marks to a bank card; Qalan pays cash. Anjeer's decisions (children never pay or earn; parent creates
  the profile and consents) are a stance against the local norm and should be written as product
  statements, as the US and KZ notes propose (W, AB, BH).
- **The state's calendar matters for the pilot.** Stage 1 is late June, stage 2 mid-July; registration
  25 May – 14 June; the ministry's online olympiads run quarterly; from 2027 a 4th-grade olympiad adds a
  district stage in the school year. A week-11 pilot (around January–February 2027 on the roadmap) lands
  in the half-year before stage 1, when families are most willing to pay.
- **Infrastructure assumptions hold.** Eduten's pilot ran 73 % on phones and much of it at home in
  Tashkent; 69 % of schools lack Wi-Fi. The child's client must be a phone-first web app with small
  payloads; the center's teacher view can assume a laptop.

### 7.1 Languages: what the home market does with four locales

- Ziyoly ships uz-Latn, ru and en; Qalan uz-Latn (Russian footer); Yooksal, TestMakon, BirPrep uz-Latn
  only; eMaktab uz-Latn and ru; the ministry's portals uz-Latn (with a Cyrillic toggle on some state
  sites). **No education platform found offers uz-Cyrl**; the exam itself is taken in Uzbek or Russian
  (Presidential schools) and the specialised-school papers are in Uzbek and English.
- Consequence for the four-locale convention (database README): uz-Cyrl is a differentiator for parents
  and teachers who read Cyrillic, not a market requirement for children; the KZ option BF (transliterate
  from uz-Latn with teacher spot-check) is the cheapest way to keep it.
- English is a *subject* in stage 2 (40 questions), so the content bank needs English-language items
  with Uzbek explanations, which is a content-language axis distinct from the UI locale (KZ glossary
  candidate "exam language").

## 9. Open questions for the owner

1. Is the reward unit a **weekly completion trophy on the Leitner queue** (EH, the Eduten shape) or a
   **daily norm in cards** (KZ BB, the Qalan shape)? Eduten is the only one with Uzbek evidence; Qalan is
   the one families here have seen advertised.
2. Does the **"no money, no bank, no partner points"** statement (EJ) apply to the parent account too, or
   only to the child? It decides whether KZ BA (a completion credit on the invoice) stays on the table.
3. Which **parent rhythm** is the default: daily line (Ziyoly), weekly digest (Newton), or parent's
   choice (EM)? And does the idle alert (EK) go to the teacher as well as the parent?
4. Is a **class-internal, opt-in ranking** (EL) acceptable at all for 9–11-year-olds under the consent
   model, or is "no ranking" the statement? Centers here are used to public lists.
5. Should Anjeer **gate anything on video** (EN)? The market does; the exposure rule argues against it.
6. How is **readiness** shown to parents (EO): cohort percentile, mock score only, or nothing until the
   center has a season of data?
7. **uz-Cyrl scope** (ER): a UI locale for adults, or full content for children as the convention says?
8. Does the **pilot move** toward the admission calendar (EQ), or does the roadmap's week count win?
9. Should Anjeer **register the child's practice record as the family's data** (export on request,
   portability, KZ BJ) explicitly in contrast with eMaktab × Hamkorbank, as a sales argument?
10. Glossary candidates when a spec needs them: *weekly trophy*, *idle alert*, *cohort percentile*,
    *exam language*, *content locale* vs *UI locale*. (Not added here; research is not authoritative.)
